\chapter{Phase 6 (v11--v13): prospective tests on new observations}\label{ch:phase6}
% =====================================================================================================

\begin{glance}{v11--v13}
\textbf{Question.} Do the two timing claims survive a \emph{prospective} test -- pipeline, thresholds, models and decision rule
frozen and committed before any new data are seen -- on NICER observations never downloaded before? And are they an artefact of
the (so far uncorrected) NICER background? \\
\textbf{Pre-declared primaries.} v11a: frozen out-of-source H$_N$+T$_N$-LR $-$ H$_N$-LR on new hard-like observations; v11b:
colour-conditional $\log_{10}\nu_c$ difference on the new observations; v12a: the same after 3C50 background correction and
screening of the v10+v11 observations; v12b: RF hard-state gain after correction (RF chosen \emph{post hoc}); v13a: frozen RF
hard-state gain on a third, disjoint set of new observations (RF chosen \emph{before} download); v13b: $\nu_c$ difference on that set. \\
\textbf{Data.} The same 66 NICER sources; 318 new observations in v11 (39.2\,GB streamed), 3C50 for 503 faint observations in v12,
238 new observations in v13 (198 after correction and screening). \\
\textbf{Verdict.} The \textbf{RF hard-state timing gain} is detected after background correction (v12b $+0.174$ [$+0.071$, $+0.300]$) and
\textbf{prospectively} on new observations with a pre-chosen RF (v13a $+0.184$ [$+0.049$, $+0.337$]); the LR gain is not detected in v11
($+0.066$). The \textbf{$\nu_c$ difference} is detected in v11 ($-0.663$, $p=0.023$) but depends on a few very hard faint observations,
and is not detected after correction (v12a $-0.514$, $p=0.073$) or prospectively (v13b $-0.427$, $p=0.21$); its size is stable at
$-0.4$ to $-0.6$\,dex. These are \textbf{observation-level} prospective tests on the \emph{same sources}, not new sources; only 16
dynamically confirmed BHs exist in the whole project.
\end{glance}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 5 measured the overall strength of the evidence but could not confirm it, because the hypotheses and pooling followed
the component results. The cleanest remaining test is prospective: freeze everything, then look at data never seen. New
dynamically confirmed BHs were exhausted (16 of the 25 BHs of \citet{Marcel2026} had been used across RXTE and NICER), and batch
access to Insight-HXMT or AstroSat data could not be verified, so the maximum independence available was \emph{new observations
of the same NICER sources}. v11 did this; its $\nu_c$ result raised the suspicion of background-dominated faint observations, so
v12 applied the official 3C50 background model \citep{Remillard2022}; v13 repeated the prospective test with the v12 pipeline and a
model (RF) chosen before the download. v14 then stopped the series (\cref{ch:synthesis}).

The frozen rule (\cref{sec:nurule}) is $\nu^*=\sqrt{\tilde\nu_\mathrm{BH}\,\tilde\nu_\mathrm{NS}}$, the geometric mean of the BH and NS median

hard-like observations are BH.

\section{Data}

\subsection{New NICER observations (v11, v13)}
\textbf{v11.} For each of the 66 v9 sources, eligible observations not among the 792 tried in v9 were split into 8 time bins and tried in a
random order (\code{default\_rng(SEED + 1000 + position)}, up to 3 per bin); sources never used before with only 1--4 eligible observations
contributed all of them (7 NS and XTE J1859+226; 19 observations). 422 source--bin combinations, 588 tries, \textbf{318 accepted}; 39.2\,GB
were streamed (raw bytes not stored; URLs and SHA256 in \file{logs/downloads.jsonl}). New hard-like observations: 33 of 9 BH sources (Cyg X-1 8,
MAXI J1820+070 6, Swift J1727.8$-$1613 5, GRS 1915+105 4, 4U 1543$-$47 3, GX 339$-$4 3, LMC X-1 2, LMC X-3 1, V4641 Sgr 1) and 110 of 33 NS sources.
Of the never-used sources, 3 NSs have hard-like observations (7); XTE J1859+226 had none that qualified. Gates: IC1 LOSO refit on the v10 data
reproduces the v10 OOF scores (maximum difference $\le1.6\times10^{-15}$); IC2 Z sources soft-like 15/16; IC3 no overlap with the v9 obsids; IC4
synthetic Lorentzians of 0.3 and 5\,Hz recovered as 0.302 and 5.10\,Hz (two-component low frequency 0.101); streamed parsing equals the v10
prefix+tail parsing (6403520101, 729,479 events).

\textbf{v13.} Eligible observations of the v9 sources not tried in v9 (792) or v11 (588), same scheme (\code{default\_rng(SEED + 2000 + position)}):
291 groups, \textbf{238 accepted}; 3C50 for the 148 faint ones (109 ok, 39 failed: 38 old gain calibration, 1 other; median $f=0.006$, one
with $f>0.20$); 198 kept after correction and screening. Hard-like: 29 observations of 8 BH sources, 45 of 19 NS sources (74). Gates: Z sources
15/16 soft-like; disjoint from v9 and v11.

\subsection{Background: 3C50 (v12)}
The 3C50 model \citep{Remillard2022} was run with HEASoft 6.37.1 \code{nibackgen3C50} (CALDB background library and index, \code{gainepoch=AUTO},
default \code{hbgcut}=0.5, \code{s0cut}=30; CALDB tarball 112,180,364 bytes, SHA256 \texttt{572954c8\dots}). A feasibility run on 3657027601 gave a
3C50 total rate of 58.0\,c\,s$^{-1}$ against 61.3 from the pipeline and a background of 0.67\,c\,s$^{-1}$. For the 503 observations of v10+v11 with
2--10\,keV $<500$\,c\,s$^{-1}$ the cleaned and unfiltered event files were downloaded, processed and deleted (spectra kept outside the repository):
362 succeeded, 141 failed -- 139 because \code{nibackgen3C50} rejects data processed with the original gain calibration
(\code{nixtiflightpi20170601v001}; recorded as return code 255, missing total spectrum, in \file{data/v12/processed/bkg3c50.csv}) and 2 with return
code 218. The background fraction $f$ (2--10\,keV) has median 0.008, 90th percentile 0.026 and 99th percentile 0.37; 9 observations exceed 0.20
(\cref{fig:p6bkg}a). Gates: IC1 3C50/pipeline total-rate ratio within [0.7, 1.3] for 93.4\% (median 0.961); IC2 all $f\in[0,1]$; IC3 Z sources 15/16
soft-like after correction.

\textbf{Correction} (\cref{sec:3c50}): fractional rms features and the state rms are multiplied by $1/(1-f_T)$; colours are recomputed from the
band counts scaled by $(1-f_A)$, $(1-f_B)$, $(1-f_C)$ (PI 200--400, 400--600, 600--1000); $\nu_c$ is not corrected; bright observations
($\ge500$\,c\,s$^{-1}$) get $f=0$. \textbf{Screening}: $f>0.20$ and 3C50 failures are excluded, leaving \textbf{534 of 684} observations; 3 change state.

\section{Models in this phase}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LR and RF with the fixed v2 settings (\S\ref{sec:lr}, \S\ref{sec:rf}), always \emph{out-of-source}: a new observation of source $s$ is scored by
the reference-data model trained without $s$, so no source contributes to the model that scores it. The permutation test, the
Lorentzian fits (\cref{eq:lorentz}: one or two zero-centred Lorentzians integrated over each octave bin, weighted least squares on
log-parameters with multiple starts, the second component kept if $\mathrm{BIC}_1-\mathrm{BIC}_2>6$, $\nu_L$ = lowest width) and the
frozen $\nu^*$ rule are defined in \cref{ch:features,ch:methods,ch:evaluation}.

\subsection{Characteristic frequency on new observations}\label{sec:p6nuc}
\Primary{} \textbf{v11b.} On the new hard-like observations (42 sources, 9 BH) the colour-conditional difference is $D=-0.663$, $p=0.0225$:
\DetectedP{} (\cref{tab:p6nuc}). Adding the log count rate as a brightness control gives $-0.709$ ($p=0.018$; on the v10 data $-0.459$,
$p=0.046$); the lowest Lorentzian width $\nu_L$ gives the same picture (median BH 0.148\,Hz, NS 0.618\,Hz; $D=-0.670$, $p=0.023$; 142 fits,
58\% with two components, 12 at the lower bound $1/128$\,Hz). Leaving out one BH gives $-0.51$ (without LMC X-1) to $-0.74$. \PostHoc{}
However, the new ``hard-like'' observations of LMC X-1, LMC X-3 and V4641 Sgr are very hard ($c_2$ 0.5--1.0) with very low $\nu_c$ (0.02--0.09\,Hz),
like background-dominated faint observations; excluding observations above the frozen v10 background-proxy threshold gives $-0.604$
($p=0.058$), and additionally excluding $c_2>0.7$ gives $-0.458$ ($p=0.19$, 7 BHs) (\file{results/v11/v11b\_post\_hoc\_screens.csv}).

\Primary{} \textbf{v12a.} After 3C50 correction and screening, $D=-0.514$, $p=0.073$: \NotDetected. Across every background treatment the
effect size is $-0.50$ to $-0.57$\,dex and only the sample size changes $p$ (\cref{tab:p6nuc}); the 141 observations that 3C50 could not
process (2017--2019, including early LMC X-1, LMC X-3 and V4641 Sgr data) are the main loss. The very hard observations suspected in v11 are
mostly not background-dominated by 3C50: of the 45 hard-like observations with $c_2>0.7$, the 15 with a 3C50 result have median $f=0.125$
(0.008--0.91); the NSs EXO 0748$-$676, 4U 1246$-$588 and 4U 1323$-$62 have $f>0.2$, while the BH observations (LMC X-3 two, GRS 1915+105 one) have
$f$ of 1--2\% (\file{results/v12/v12\_very\_hard\_obs.csv}). \PostHoc{} 3C50 filters high-background intervals for its own spectra, whereas the
timing features use all GTIs, so $f$ may underestimate the background in the timing data -- the motivation for the GTI-restricted check
in v13.

\Primary{} \textbf{v13b.} Prospectively, $D=-0.427$, $p=0.209$ (27 sources, 8 BH): \NotDetected{}; without screening or correction $-0.520$
($p=0.081$); with features recomputed on the 3C50 intervals $-0.300$ ($p=0.41$); leaving out one BH $-0.31$ to $-0.50$.

\subsection{The frozen rule and never-used sources}\label{sec:p6rule}

\section{Interpretation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{What the data support directly.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item With pipeline, background treatment, models and model type frozen and the observations new, timing raises the hard-state BH/NS
separation by $\approx0.18$ in observation-level AUC with an interval excluding 0 (v13a). The RF gain has been stable across five NICER
analyses (+0.17 to +0.25).
\item The LR gain was not confirmed prospectively in v11; it was detected in v13. LR's calibration drifts between epochs (never-used NSs
at 0.51--0.60), RF's does not.
\item The colour-conditional $\nu_c$ difference keeps its sign and size ($-0.3$ to $-0.7$\,dex) in every NICER analysis, but it is detected only
once (v11, where it depends on a few very hard faint observations) and not after background correction (v12) or prospectively (v13).
\item NICER backgrounds are mostly small (median $f\approx0.8\%$); background correction does not change the size of either effect.
\item The frozen $\nu^*$ rule identifies BH sources (16/17 over v11 and v13) but has poor NS specificity.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Possible explanations (not verified).} The timing information acts non-linearly with colour (e.g.\ strong low-frequency variability
is BH-like only in a certain colour range), which RF captures and LR captures unstably. The label-free colour residualisation is
conservative when BHs cluster in colour; with 8 BH sources the $\nu_c$ test has little power. The RF gain may use combinations of
$T_1$--$T_3$ beyond $\nu_c$.

\textbf{What would be needed.} New BH sources -- future transients or other missions -- tested with the same frozen procedure; that would
be a source-level confirmation, which this project could not make.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Deviations and caveats}\label{sec:p6caveats}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Written after earlier results, self-approved}; in v12 the choice of RF as primary was made after v9--v11.
\item \textbf{Same sources}: the prospective tests are independent at the observation level only; new and old observations of a source may
belong to the same outburst.
\item \textbf{v11}: observation-level parallelism reduced from 3 to 2 (memory), six concurrent 64-MB range requests per file; $\nu^*$ computed and
committed before any new data, as pre-registered; the background/very-hard sensitivity analysis of v11b is post hoc and does not change
the verdict; raw bytes not stored (re-downloadable with the logged URLs and SHA256).
\item \textbf{v12}: 3C50 cannot process data with the original gain calibration; a 2018 gain-epoch fallback did not work and was removed, the
failures excluded as pre-registered, and an ``include uncorrected'' sensitivity added before modelling; 3C50 jobs 3 $\to$ 6 (speed only); 3C50
estimates a mean background, does not model its time variation, uses its own GTI filtering, and its library does not cover data after
May 2023.
\item \textbf{v13}: no analytical deviation; after an interruption the analyst wrongly believed the faint observations' event files had been
deleted (they were kept; recorded in the decision log).
\item \textbf{No Eddington-ratio control}: the MINBAR source table has no distances; the count rate is the only brightness proxy.
\item \textbf{Lorentzian fits}: only 13 octave bins; 12 widths at the lower bound.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap and permutation} keep predictions and features fixed.
\end{itemize}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Takeaway}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On genuinely new NICER observations, with everything frozen in advance, low-frequency variability adds BH/NS information inside the hard
state ($+0.18$ in AUC, RF, prospectively detected); this is the most robust positive result of the project, but it is an observation-level
confirmation on the same 16 dynamically confirmed BHs. The lower characteristic frequency of BHs at fixed colour is consistent in size
but not robustly significant once backgrounds are treated and data are new. The series stopped here because further versions could only
re-use the same sources (\cref{ch:synthesis}).
