\chapter{Phase 3 (v5--v6): fast variability as a second axis}\label{ch:phase3}
% =====================================================================================================

\begin{glance}{v5--v6}
\textbf{Question.} Phase 2 showed that a single 5--25\,keV spectrum is exhausted at two colours, with hard-state BHs and
hard NSs inseparable. Does the aperiodic variability between $1/128$ and 4\,Hz, measured from the same RXTE
observations, add information \emph{at fixed colour}, and does that hold for sources the model has never seen? \\
\textbf{Pre-declared primaries.} v5: H+T-LR $-$ H-LR on the 31 v2 sources (S1, LOSO); v6a: a model \emph{frozen} after
training on S1, H$_R$+T-LR $-$ H$_R$-LR, evaluated on 23 external sources (E: 4 BH, 19 NS) never used in training.
Both judged on source BA and source AUC. \\
\textbf{Data.} RXTE/PCA Standard-1 (0.125\,s, no energy resolution), no new download for v5; v6 adds 12 sources
(2 HMXB BHs, 6 AMXPs, 4 slow pulsars as a stress set; 180 observations). \\
\textbf{Verdict.} Both primaries \textbf{not detected} under the strict rule, for different reasons: in v5 the
intervals touch 0 (source BA $+0.113$ [$-0.021$, $+0.277$], AUC $+0.065$ [0.000, $+0.179$]); in v6a both models already
reach source AUC 1.000 on E and the BA gain $+0.105$ has lower limit 0.000. Every non-saturating metric points the same
way (v6a: Brier $-0.059$ [$-0.093$, $-0.027$], log loss $-0.127$ [$-0.217$, $-0.044$]). The colour-conditional
permutation test is significant on the 31 v2 sources (T1$^*$ Holm $p=0.0024$) but \textbf{does not replicate} on E.
The new classifier CCTLR does not beat the linear H+T-LR. Both pre-registrations were written after all earlier results
and self-approved under the user's standing instruction.
\end{glance}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Phase 1--2 errors are hard-state sources whose colours overlap. In the hard state both BHs and NSs show strong
band-limited noise, but at a given colour the amplitude and the characteristic frequencies need not be the same: the
characteristic frequencies of the broad-band noise scale with the size of the emitting region and therefore, at fixed
state, are expected to be lower for more massive compact objects (a possible explanation, not tested here). The
well-known high-frequency difference (NS power persisting above $\sim500$\,Hz while BH power falls off above
10--50\,Hz; \citealp{SunyaevRevnivtsev2000}) is not accessible with Standard-1 data. The PCA Standard-1 mode, however, is
available for every observation already processed: 128-s rows of 1024 counts in 0.125-s bins per PCU, giving
frequencies from $1/128$ to 4\,Hz. The rms of BH transients changes with state \citep{MunozDarias2011}; whether
$<4$\,Hz variability separates BHs from NSs \emph{beyond} colour had no clear literature answer, so v5 was declared
exploratory. v6 then asked the confirmatory question with frozen models on new sources and added better estimators,
a structured classifier, correct inference for clustered data and usable outputs (conformal sets, observation budgets).

\subsection{Implementation checks}
All pre-declared gates passed before any model was trained (\file{logs/v5/test\_v5lib.log},
\file{logs/v6/test\_v6lib.log}, \file{results/v5/v5\_implementation\_checks.csv}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Synthetic recovery (v5).} A common sinusoid of fractional rms 0.2 at 0.05, 0.5 and 3\,Hz was recovered in
T1, T2, T3 as 0.198, 0.198, 0.199 (cospectrum) and 0.199, 0.198, 0.198 (auto-power); pure Poisson gives median $|T|<0.02$
in every band ($-0.006$, $-0.008$, $-0.016$); with 50\% background the T2 rms is 0.199.
\item \textbf{Cross-check on real data.} For the 276 S1 observations with 20--500\,c\,s$^{-1}$\,PCU$^{-1}$, the cospectrum T2
and the classical auto-power-minus-Poisson T2 agree with Spearman $\rho=0.970$ (gate 0.90).
\item \textbf{Plausibility.} Within each of the 7 BH sources, the total rms correlates positively with the hard colour
(Spearman 0.18 for GRO J1655$-$40 to 0.88 for XTE J1550$-$564, median 0.61; \file{results/v5/v5\_check\_bh\_rms\_hardness.csv}),
as expected from higher rms in the hard state.
\item \textbf{All-pairs estimator (v6b).} Under pure Poisson noise the all-pairs estimator is unbiased and its variance is
31\%, 33\% and 41\% lower than that of the A/B split for 3, 4 and 5 PCUs. For equal count rates the expected reduction
is $1-n_An_B/\binom{n}{2}$, i.e.\ 33\%, 33\% and 40\% (the A/B variance scales as $1/(n_An_B)$, the all-pairs
variance as $1/\binom n2$ because distinct pairs are uncorrelated under independent Poisson noise). A sinusoid of rms 0.2 is
recovered as 0.188--0.199 with 1 or 4 PCUs; $\nu_c$ of a 0.05, 0.5 and 3\,Hz sinusoid is 0.045, 0.705 and 2.81\,Hz (within
one logarithmic bin). On real data T$^*$ agrees with the v5 T (Spearman 0.977, 0.977, 0.968 for T1--T3, 7,228 observations)
and is more reliable in odd/even-row split halves (\cref{tab:p3reliab}). Missing values drop from 942 to 341.
\item \textbf{Permutation test calibration.} Under a synthetic null the fraction of $p<0.05$ was 0.033 (gate $\le0.12$); under
a synthetic alternative $p<0.01$. Conformal LOSO coverage at $\varepsilon=0.2$ was 0.816 (gate $\ge0.78$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Timing as a new axis on the v2 sources (v5)}\label{sec:p3v5}

source BA 0.795 $\to$ 0.908, source AUC 0.923 $\to$ 0.988, observation BA 0.776 $\to$ 0.878. By the pre-declared rule

both models rank all sources perfectly the difference cannot be positive. Timing alone is much weaker than colour
(T-LR source AUC 0.744): it complements colour, it does not replace it.

\subsection{Robustness: nuisance variables and frequency bands (v6h)}\label{sec:p3robust}
\Descriptive{} Timing amplitudes depend on brightness (Poisson noise), the number of PCUs, the background fraction and the
epoch. Adding these nuisance variables $N$ to the colours does not help detectably (H+N $-$ H log loss $-0.063$

\section{Interpretation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{What the data support directly.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Both pre-declared primaries are \textbf{not detected}: in v5 because the intervals touch 0, in v6a because both models
already reach source AUC 1.000 on the external sources and the BA interval has lower limit 0.
\item On external sources the timing features correct the \emph{threshold miscalibration} of the colour-only model: five
NSs at 0.56--0.67 move to 0.25--0.34 (one NS moves the other way), and the non-saturating metrics -- observation BA, Brier
score and log loss -- all improve with intervals excluding 0.
\item The timing information sits at 0.016--1\,Hz (T1, T2); 1--4\,Hz adds nothing. It survives control for brightness,
number of PCUs, background fraction and year.
\item ``At fixed colour, BHs have stronger low-frequency variability and a lower characteristic frequency'' holds on the 31
v2 sources (Holm $p=0.002$--$0.04$) but \textbf{does not replicate} on the 23 external sources.
\item The all-pairs estimator is more precise and reduces missing values from 942 to 341; the new CCTLR classifier does not
beat the simple linear H+T-LR.
\item With timing, source-level conformal sets keep their nominal coverage on external sources; with colour only they do not.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Possible explanations (not verified).}
\begin{itemize}
\item The BH part of E differs from the training BHs: two are high-mass X-ray binaries (Cyg X-1; LMC X-3, mostly in the soft
state), XTE J1859+226 is entirely in gain epoch 4. With 4 BHs the permutation test has little power, so ``not replicated''
may be lack of power; the S1 effect may also be driven by a few sources (XTE J1118+480, GX 339$-$4).
\item On E the timing features mainly pull NSs down while slightly lowering the BH scores, which is consistent with weaker
low-frequency variability of NSs at a given colour but suggests that the BH side of the relation is unstable.
\item CCTLR's class-conditional model (linear in colour, Student-$t$ residuals) may be mis-specified for 31 sources; a
linear LR is more stable at this sample size.
\item 4U 1626$-$67 rises because its coherent 0.13\,Hz pulsation is counted as broad-band variability; for strongly pulsating
sources the pulse frequency would have to be removed first.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Physically.} The direction found on S1 -- more variability at 0.016--1\,Hz and a centroid frequency lower by a
factor $\approx2$ for BHs at the same colour -- is what one expects if the characteristic frequencies of the broad-band
noise scale inversely with the mass of the compact object, but the frequencies probed here (below 4\,Hz) are far below
those of the canonical BH/NS timing differences, and the effect did not replicate. The robust part of the result is
practical rather than physical: timing calibrates the colour classifier on new sources.

\textbf{Hypotheses that need more data.} More external confirmed BHs (especially hard-state LMXB transients); an
explicit spectral-state split and higher frequencies from event-mode data (taken up in Phase 4); another instrument with
new observations (NICER, Phase 5); full power-spectrum fits with Lorentzian characteristic frequencies (Phase 6); a
confirmation in which a proper scoring rule is the pre-declared primary.

\section{Deviations and caveats}\label{sec:p3caveats}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Written after earlier results, self-approved}: v5 after v1--v4, v6 after v1--v5, both designed and approved by
the analyst under the user's standing instruction, without external review; timing was proposed to address the known
colour overlap.
\item \textbf{v5 unit test}: the ``PCU switched off within a row'' test case was wrong (switch-off at bin 900 leaves counts in
the last 128-bin block, so by the rule the PCU is still on); the test was corrected before any real feature was computed.
The rule itself did not change.
\item \textbf{v5 reporting}: \code{frac\_first\_better} (fraction of resamples with a positive difference) is reported; it was
already an output of \code{v4lib.paired\_bootstrap}, not a new analysis.
\item \textbf{v6}: no change of source list, bands, estimators, models or decision rules; all exclusions followed the rules
(\file{data/v6/source\_verification.csv}). A figure-annotation bug was fixed before the first run; \file{run\_v6.ps1} clears
\code{XRB\_VERSION} with \code{\$env:XRB\_VERSION = \$null} (same effect).
\item \textbf{Saturation}: the headline metrics saturated on E (AUC = 1), which the design did not anticipate; the proper-score
improvements are descriptive.
\item \textbf{Small BH samples}: 7 BHs in S1, 9 in S2, 4 in E (2 in E\textsubscript{new}); one source such as XTE J1118+480 changes the source BA
by about 0.07.
\item \textbf{Not independent}: S2 and S3 share the S1 sources; the 15 v4b2 sources in E had been seen by the analyst in other
models' results (hence E\textsubscript{new}).
\item \textbf{Instrument}: Standard-1 has no energy resolution (rms over $\approx$2--60\,keV, colours in 5--25\,keV) and a 4\,Hz
upper limit; the background is assumed equal for all PCUs and taken from the Standard-2 background model; the cospectrum
amplitude is not deadtime-corrected (a few per cent at high rates); 34 S1 observations (7 BH) with one PCU are imputed.
\item \textbf{Population differences}: training BHs are all LMXB transients, two external BHs are HMXBs; the external NSs
are mostly AMXPs; XTE J1751$-$305, IGR J17511$-$3057 and IGR J17498$-$2921 lie 0.3--0.7$^\circ$ from each other or from v4b2
sources and may contaminate each other in the $\approx1^\circ$ PCA field.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap} intervals keep the predictions fixed and do not include fitting variability.
\end{itemize}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Takeaway}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Low-frequency variability is the first quantity in this project that adds to the two colours: every point estimate moves
in its favour, on the 31 training sources and, with frozen models, on 23 unseen ones. By the strict pre-declared rule
neither primary is detected -- in v6a because the headline metrics saturate -- and the colour-conditional mechanism
(lower characteristic frequency for BHs) does not replicate externally. What does hold externally is calibration: timing
moves the colour-only classifier's misplaced NSs back below the threshold and keeps conformal coverage. Phase 4 makes the
state dependence explicit (does the BH/NS separation fail precisely in the hard state?), adds event-mode frequencies above
4\,Hz, rechecks burst contamination against the MINBAR catalogue, and tests long-term colours on an independent all-sky
instrument (MAXI).
