\chapter{Phase 1 (v1--v3): spectral baselines}\label{ch:phase1}
% =====================================================================================================

\begin{glance}{v1--v3}
\textbf{Question.} Can one RXTE/PCA 5--25\,keV spectrum classify the compact object of a source never seen in training,
and where in the spectrum is the information? \\
\textbf{Pre-declared primaries.} v1: none formal (LOSO evaluation of A and B; leakage diagnostic); v2: paired B$-$A
difference (source bootstrap); v3a: H+B $-$ H (``is there information beyond two colours?''), judged on observation BA,
source BA and source AUC. \\
\textbf{Data.} RXTE/PCA Standard-2 Standard Products, gain epoch 5 (2000--2011); v1: 8 sources (4 BH, 4 NS) $\times$ 15
observations; v2/v3: 31 sources (7 BH, 24 NS), 456 observations, 1842\,ks; v3c: 14 BH candidates (210 observations,
prediction only). \\
\textbf{Verdict.} Better than chance on unseen sources (source BA 0.80--0.87, source AUC 0.88--0.95 for the balanced
models), but \emph{the usable information is in two count-space colours}: the full shape (H+B $-$ H), 3--5\,keV and
intensity add nothing \textbf{detectable}. Errors concentrate on hard-state BHs and hard NSs, which overlap in colour.
\end{glance}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Published classifiers of RXTE spectra reported 87--94\% accuracy \citep{Pattnaik2021,Garg2026}, but with either
sigma-clipped source accuracies or observation-wise random splits, and with BH candidates labelled as BHs. Neither
published its processed spectra. Phase 1 rebuilt the data from the HEASARC Standard Products, used only confirmed labels,
and asked the question in its strict form: how well can an \emph{unseen source} be classified, and is the information more
than a simple hardness measure?

\subsection{Instrument, mode, band and epoch}
RXTE/PCA Standard-2 Standard Products (\cref{sec:pca}), 5--25\,keV on the 45-bin grid (v3b: 2.883--25.008\,keV), net
count rate per keV per PCU after background subtraction and deadtime correction (v1: good-xenon lower bound; v2: full

\subsection{Data quality}
All integrity checks of \file{06\_dataset\_checks.py} pass (unique ObsIDs, labels consistent, all values finite, units
\texttt{count}, BACKSCAL ratio 1, header position $\le0.045^\circ$ from the source). Non-positive net bins: 7 of 5400 in v1
(3 observations, all above 21\,keV), 55 of 20\,520 in v2 (18 observations, high-energy end of soft spectra); kept as is.
Minimum S/N: 34.9 (v1), 19.1 (v2). Deadtime (v2 recipe): median 1.38\%, maximum 8.8\%. Exposure: 401.6\,ks (v1);
1841.8\,ks in v2, of which 267.6\,ks BH and 1574.2\,ks NS. \Cref{fig:p1diag,fig:p1spectra} show the per-source
distributions of the quantities that define the sample.

\subsection{The pilot (v1, 8 sources)}
\Descriptive{} With 4 BH and 4 NS sources, the observation-level BA under LOSO was 0.70 (A-LR), 0.69 (A-RF), 0.76 (B-LR)
and 0.74 (B-RF); 6--7 of 8 sources were classified correctly, but every model had one or two sources that were mostly
wrong, and \emph{which} sources failed changed with the representation (\cref{tab:p1v1persource}). The Dummy scored BA = 0 for
the reason explained in \cref{sec:dummy}. The source bootstrap (added in v2) gives source BA 0.75 $[0.50,1.00]$ (A-LR),
0.875 $[0.625,1.00]$ (A-RF), 0.75 $[0.375,1.00]$ (B-LR, B-RF), and the B$-$A observation-BA differences +0.058
$[-0.158,+0.217]$ (LR) and +0.050 $[-0.250,+0.283]$ (RF): eight sources cannot distinguish A from B
(\file{results/bootstrap\_source\_level.csv}). The leaky observation-wise split gave 0.80--0.89 (\cref{fig:leakage}).

\section{Interpretation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{Statistically.} On 31 sources the spectral classifiers are far better than chance, but every comparison that asks
whether \emph{more} spectral information helps is not detected: the full shape adds nothing to two colours, 3--5\,keV
adds nothing, and 45 intensity bins add nothing to one intensity number. The intervals are wide because only 7 BH sources
exist, so ``not detected'' is not ``absent''; but the point estimates are themselves near zero or negative, and the B and
H models agree on 88--93\% of individual observations. The leaky split would have reported 0.77--0.88 BA.

\textbf{Physically.} In soft states the BH disc is cool and accompanied by a power-law tail, so the soft colour is low
and the hard colour relatively high; NS soft states have a boundary-layer/surface component that cuts off at 10--20\,keV.
These two populations separate in the colour plane, as in \citet{DoneGierlinski2003} (whose colours were response-corrected
and defined differently; no formal comparison was made). In hard states both are Comptonisation-dominated and their
5--25\,keV shapes are similar, so the colours overlap -- the errors are the hard-state BHs (XTE J1118+480, the hard
observations of GX 339$-$4, XTE J1550$-$564) and hard NSs (AMXPs, KS 1731$-$260). GRS 1915+105, long near Eddington with a
curved soft spectrum, resembles Z/atoll soft states in shape. The \citet{Pattnaik2021} LOSO result (87\%) is of the same
order as this project's; the new finding is that two hardness ratios reproduce it, which suggests that their Random Forest
also learned hardness (not tested directly).

\section{Deviations and caveats}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H colours linear instead of $\log_{10}$} (before any v2 training; two BH spectra with slightly negative
16--25\,keV sums).
\item \PostHoc{} \textbf{B$-$H and A$-$HI paired differences} added after the v2 results (they answer the pre-declared
question ``does the model only learn hardness?'').
\item \textbf{Output-path bug}: \file{08\_error\_analysis.py} overwrote two v1 figures during the v2 run; restored from git and fixed (no effect on numbers).
\item \textbf{XSPEC invocation}: commands are passed as a script file because inline passing through \texttt{wsl.exe}
expanded \texttt{\$xspec\_tclout} to an empty string (fixed before the comparison).
\item \textbf{v3c}: candidate list drafted from memory, verified and frozen before download; one catalogue-name fix; two
candidates without catalogues skipped by rule.
\item \textbf{v2 source rule executed incompletely} (found in v4b2): 13 further Galloway+2008 NSs had catalogues and
$\ge10$ eligible pointings but were missed; the v1--v3 numbers were kept and describe a smaller NS sample (\cref{ch:phase2}).
\item \textbf{Reproducibility}: v3a re-ran the v2 models exactly (\file{results/v3/v3a\_reproduction\_check.csv}: maximum
score difference $1.1\times10^{-16}$ for LR and $2.2\times10^{-16}$ for RF, 0 label changes); a second machine (macOS) reproduced
\file{features.npz} to $\le10^{-16}$ and RF source means to $\le0.001$.
% CHECK: report_v3_zh-TW.md says the LR reproduction difference was <= 8e-10; that number refers to the cloud-sandbox
% run (decision_log: sandbox vs local 7.8e-10). The committed results/v3/v3a_reproduction_check.csv (local re-run vs v2)
% shows 1.1e-16.
\item \textbf{Caveats}: count space (no response unfolding; PCU-combination tilt of $\pm10\%$ at 25\,keV); background-model
systematics not included; Standard Products use generic screening; no per-observation state labels in this phase;
bootstrap intervals do not include refitting variability.
\end{itemize}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Takeaway}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A single 5--25\,keV RXTE spectrum classifies unseen sources better than chance, but what it offers is captured by two
count-space colours; the failures are hard-state BHs and hard NSs that overlap in those colours. Phase 2 asks whether this
ceiling is a property of the models or of the data: more flexible algorithms, more observations, more sources and a wider
energy band are tested against the two-colour LR.
