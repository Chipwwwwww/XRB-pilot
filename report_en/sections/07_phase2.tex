\chapter{Phase 2 (v4): is the spectral information saturated?}\label{ch:phase2}
% =====================================================================================================

\begin{glance}{v4 (v4a, burst check, v4b3, v4b2, v4b1)}
\textbf{Question.} Phase 1 found that two count-space colours carry all the detectable information in a 5--25\,keV
spectrum. Is that a limit of the \emph{models} (more flexible algorithms), of the \emph{data quality} (type-I burst
contamination), of the \emph{band} (HEXTE 25--60\,keV) or of the \emph{sample} (more sources from earlier gain epochs;
all observations of each source)? \\
\textbf{Single baseline.} The v2 two-colour logistic regression H-LR (source BA 0.795 [0.589, 0.958], source AUC 0.923
[0.804, 0.994], observation BA 0.776). ``Improvement'' $\Leftrightarrow$ the paired 95\% interval lies entirely above 0. \\
\textbf{Pre-declared primary.} v4a part 1e: a fully nested procedure that chooses algorithm, representation,
hyper-parameters and threshold inside the training sources, compared with H-LR. Each v4b sub-phase and the burst check
also had one pre-declared main comparison. \\
\textbf{Data.} v4a, burst check: the v2 sample (31 sources, 456 observations); v4b3: the 396 observations with HEXTE
cluster-B products; v4b2: 46 sources (9 BH, 37 NS), 677 observations, gain epochs 2--5; v4b1: all 7,949 eligible epoch-5
observations of the 31 v2 sources. \\
\textbf{Verdict.} Every primary comparison: \textbf{not detected}. Nested automatic selection was \emph{worse} in point
estimate (source BA $-0.062$, source AUC $-0.173$); bursts, HEXTE colours and 17$\times$ more observations changed
nothing. The only detected difference is a threshold effect on the extended 46-source sample (RF source BA
$+0.068$ [$+0.014$, $+0.122$] over the same-sample H-LR, source AUC not different). \textbf{The v4 pre-registration
was written after all v1--v3 results were known.}
\end{glance}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 1 left one negative result that could have several causes. ``The full spectral shape adds nothing to two colours''
(H+B $-$ H not detected, \cref{sec:p1results}) can mean that the 5--25\,keV spectrum really contains no further
source-transferable information, or that the classifiers were too rigid (LR, RF with fixed settings), that observations
were contaminated by thermonuclear bursts (whose spectra are not those of the persistent emission), that the
informative part of the spectrum lies above 25\,keV (high-energy cut-off of NS Comptonisation versus the unbroken BH
power law), or that 31 sources (7 BH) are simply too few. Phase 2 tests each explanation separately against the same
fixed baseline, H-LR, so that its answer is ``the ceiling is (or is not) a property of the data''. The analyses are
grouped by theme below: \emph{flexible algorithms} (v4a), \emph{data quality} (burst check), \emph{wider band} (v4b3)
and \emph{more data} (v4b2: more sources and earlier epochs; v4b1: all observations of the same sources).

\subsection{Type-I burst screening}\label{sec:p2bursts}
The detector is defined in \cref{sec:bursts}: Standard-1 good-xenon counts summed over PCUs, 1-s bins inside the spectral
GTIs, candidates $>4\sigma$ above the observation mean (after \citealp{Galloway2008}), merged within 30\,s, and an
automatic shape rule (peak $\ge1.5\times$ persistent, rise $\le10$\,s, half-decay 3--300\,s, $\ge3$ adjacent bins above
25\% amplitude). Every candidate was plotted and inspected (\cref{fig:p2bursts}).

\begin{itemize}
\item 187 of the 456 observations had at least one candidate (410 candidates in total); the automatic rule flagged 61
observations (\file{results/v4/burst\_flags.csv}).
\item Visual inspection (\file{results/v4/burst\_visual.csv}): of the 61 automatic flags, \textbf{12 are BH observations}
(the periodic flares of GRS 1915+105 and second-scale flares of hard-state BHs: GRS 1915+105 2, GX 339$-$4 2,
XTE J1118+480 4, XTE J1550$-$564 4) and one is NS noise, so 48 are real bursts; the rule missed 10 further observations
(8 clear bursts with GTI truncation, rise $>10$\,s or half-decay $<3$\,s, and 2 ``possible''). The visual set has 58
observations, all NS (3 marked ``possible'').
\item As pre-registered, the \emph{automatic} set defines the main exclusion (395 observations kept) and the visual set is
the sensitivity analysis (398 kept). No source was removed; 4U 1916$-$053 kept 4 of its 6 observations (below the
pre-declared 5, kept and recorded) and the dipper 4U 1323$-$619 lost 9 of 15 (\file{results/v4/burst\_exclusion\_counts.csv}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{HEXTE cluster B (v4b3)}
The HEXTE Standard Products contain source and background spectra of both clusters and their responses (\cref{sec:hexte}).
Cluster A has no background products after July 2006, and cluster B stopped rocking on 2009-12-14 (MJD 55179.6736), so
the pre-registered rule used cluster B only (net = \code{s1} $-$ \code{b1}, scaled by exposure and \code{BACKSCAL}) and
gave no HEXTE features to observations after that date: 60 of the 456 v2 observations (6 BH, 54 NS, 18 sources). The
remaining 396 observations all had products; every header has \code{DEADAPP = T} (deadtime already applied) and every
spectrum uses \file{xh97mar20c\_pwb013.rmf}. The two colours are
\begin{equation}
X_1=\frac{F_\mathrm{HEXTE}(25\text{--}40)}{F_\mathrm{PCA}(16\text{--}25)},\qquad
X_2=\frac{F_\mathrm{HEXTE}(40\text{--}60)}{F_\mathrm{HEXTE}(25\text{--}40)}
\label{eq:p2hexte}
\end{equation}
(count space; $X_1$ is a cross-instrument ratio used only as a feature). A colour whose denominator has $S/N<3$ is set
missing: $X_1$ in 3 observations (2 BH, 1 NS), $X_2$ in 144 (23 BH, 121 NS); one observation (92023-03-37-00) had zero
background exposure. Missing values are replaced by the training-fold median and flagged by a 0/1 indicator
(\cref{sec:missing}). The class distributions (\cref{fig:p2hexte}) overlap almost completely.

\subsection{Source-list verification and earlier gain epochs (v4b2)}
The v4b2 selection (\file{scripts/20\_v4b2\_select.py}) matched every \citet{Galloway2008} burster (48) and every BlackCAT
dynamical BH (18) to the 291 RXTE MissionLongData catalogues by \emph{position}: CDS Sesame/SIMBAD coordinates against the
median pointing of each catalogue, accepted within $0.1^\circ$ (\file{data/v4b2/source\_verification.csv}). Results:
\begin{itemize}
\item 46 of the 66 objects have a matching catalogue; 20 do not (9 BHs, e.g.\ GRS 1009$-$45, A 0620$-$00, V404 Cyg, and
11 NSs, including the Rapid Burster and IGR J17473$-$2721, whose catalogues exist but point $>0.1^\circ$ away).
\item \textbf{The v2 source rule had been executed incompletely}: 13 NSs have catalogues and $\ge10$ eligible pointings but
were not in the v2 list (4U 0513$-$40, 4U 0919$-$54, XTE J1709$-$267, XTE J1710$-$281, SAX J1747.0$-$2853, 1A 1744$-$361,
SAX J1748.9$-$2021 in NGC 6440, EXO 1745$-$248 in Terzan 5, SAX J1750.8$-$2900, GRS 1747$-$312, XTE J1759$-$220, Ser X-1,
4U 2129+12 in M15). The v2 results were not changed; this is a limitation of Phases 1--2 (\cref{sec:p2caveats}).
\item With all gain epochs allowed (MJD $<55931$; epoch boundaries MJD 50163.7729, 50188.9618, 51259.7340, 51677.0000 from
the HEASARC energy--channel table), two BHs become eligible: XTE J1859+226 (112 eligible pointings, all in epoch 4) and
V4641 Sgr (11, epochs 4--5).
\end{itemize}
The 456 v2 observations were kept unchanged; each of the 15 new sources contributed 15 observations (V4641 Sgr: 11) drawn
with the v2 time-stratified rule over the whole mission. All 221 new observations passed the quality rules
(\file{results/v4b2/attrition\_download.csv}), giving 46 sources (9 BH, 37 NS) and 677 observations (131 BH).
\Cref{tab:p2v4b2new} lists the new sources with their epochs. The new representation R divides each bin by the expected
counts of a $\Gamma=2$, $N_\mathrm{H}=0$ power law folded through the observation's response (\code{pl2},
\cref{sec:pl2}); H$_R$ are the colours of R (\cref{eq:HR}).

\subsection{All observations of the same sources (v4b1)}
For the 31 v2 sources, every eligible epoch-5 pointing was processed with the v2 rules: 7,975 candidates, 7,949 accepted
(2,369 BH, 5,580 NS); 26 excluded (14 without Standard-2 products at HEASARC, 7 high deadtime, 5 short exposure;
\file{data/v4b1/observations.csv}). Per source the counts range from 6 (4U 1916$-$053) to 1,329 (4U 1636$-$53);
GRS 1915+105 has 1,031 (\cref{tab:p2v4b1persource}). The raw files ($\approx2$\,GB, $\approx30{,}000$ files) were stored
outside the repository (\code{XRB\_EXTERNAL\_RAW}); download threads were raised from 4 to 6. \textbf{The effective sample
size is still 31 sources (7 BH)}: the number of observations grows $17\times$, but the source-level power hardly changes.

\section{Results}\label{sec:p2results}

All intervals are 95\% class-stratified source-bootstrap intervals (2000 resamples, seed 42, fixed OOF predictions, no
refitting; \cref{sec:bootstrap}); every difference is paired with the baseline on the same resamples.

\subsection{Flexible algorithms (v4a)}\label{sec:p2v4a}

\textbf{Reproduction (1a).} The v4 code reproduced the v2 OOF scores of LR and RF on all four representations: maximum
absolute difference $1.1\times10^{-16}$ (RF on H: $2.2\times10^{-16}$), 0 label changes
(\file{results/v4/v4a\_reproduction\_check.csv}); the v4 paired bootstrap also reproduces the v3a intervals bit for bit
(\file{scripts/test\_v4lib.py}).

\textbf{Primary (1e).} \Primary{} \Cref{tab:p2primary} gives the result: nested automatic selection reached source BA
0.732 and source AUC 0.750, \emph{below} H-LR in both point estimates; the differences are \NotDetected{} (both intervals
contain 0, and both lie mostly below 0). Its OOF scores correlate with those of H-LR at Spearman 0.673 and agree in
label on 75.7\% of observations (\file{results/v4/v4a\_agreement.csv}).

\section{Interpretation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{What the data support directly.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item On the 31 confirmed sources, no pre-declared comparison beats H-LR: not algorithm choice (v4a 1e), not HEXTE
colours (v4b3), not the extended sample evaluated on the original observations (v4b2), and not 17 times more
observations per source (v4b1, whose 31 source decisions are identical to those of v2).
\item Letting the training data choose the algorithm, representation and threshold made the outer result \emph{worse}
in point estimate ($-0.06$ in source BA, $-0.17$ in source AUC). The inner AUC of 0.96--1.00 against an outer 0.75 shows
how large the selection optimism is with 30 training sources.
\item Type-I burst contamination does not drive the Phase 1 conclusions. An automatic Standard-1 burst detector confuses BH
variability with bursts (12 of 61 flags) and misses slow bursts, so visual confirmation is necessary.
\item HEXTE cluster B adds nothing detectable to the two PCA colours on this sample.
\item On 46 sources, RF beats the same-sample H-LR in source BA but not in source AUC: it places the threshold better
for the larger NS population (37/37 against 32/37) without ranking sources better.
\item Four descriptive v4b1 combinations with intensity reach source BA 0.929 (only XTE J1118+480 wrong) without a
source-AUC difference; the same models with 15 observations per source did not.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Possible explanations (not verified).}
\begin{itemize}
\item The two colours already compress what is source-transferable in 5--25\,keV; flexible models add variance, not
information, on 31 sources.
\item SVM-RBF on intensity A won the inner selection probably because intensity is tied to source identity (distance,
luminosity, RXTE target selection), so inside 30 training sources it can ``recognise'' groups of sources, which does not
transfer to a new source.
\item HEXTE cluster B has a small effective area; 40--60\,keV is below $S/N=3$ in 144 of 396 observations. A
high-energy cut-off difference between hard-state BHs and NSs may need much higher $S/N$ (stacking, or NuSTAR).
\item The extended-sample RF advantage agrees with the ``threshold problem'' seen in v3: when many more NSs enter, LR gives
NSs near the boundary scores slightly above 0.5.
\item The v4b1 intensity result may arise because more observations per source cover each source's count-rate range more
completely; this is a selection property of the sample, not necessarily spectral information. v4a points the other way
(intensity models chosen inside, failing outside), so the transferability of intensity is open.
\item Observation-weighted (balanced) H-LR is worse probably because sources with very many observations
(4U 1636$-$53, GRS 1915+105) dominate the boundary; source-equal weights avoid this.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Hypotheses that need more data.} More dynamically confirmed BHs (v4b2 added only two); a pre-defined spectral-state
split (do BH and NS separate within the hard state? -- taken up in Phase 4); stacked HEXTE or NuSTAR spectra above
25\,keV; intensity on an independent source sample with a different distance distribution and instrument (taken up with
MAXI in Phase 4).

\textbf{Physically.} The decision boundaries (\cref{fig:p2boundaries}) agree wherever the populations are separated and
disagree only in the hard-colour region where hard-state BHs and hard NSs (atolls in the island state, AMXPs) overlap.
Both are dominated there by thermal Comptonisation with similar electron temperatures in 5--25\,keV; the difference
expected from the literature -- on average lower Comptonisation temperatures (lower cut-off energies) in NSs, attributed
to the additional seed photons from the surface \citep{Burke2017} -- lies mostly above 25\,keV, which is why HEXTE was tried; at HEXTE's sensitivity it is not visible in single observations. The
quantity that differs fundamentally (a surface or not) is not imprinted on a single PCA spectrum strongly enough to be
learned from 7--9 BH sources, which motivates the turn to timing in Phase 3.

\section{Deviations and caveats}\label{sec:p2caveats}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Written after v1--v3}: the v4 pre-registration was written after all earlier results; every question
targets a known result.
\item \PostHoc{} \textbf{Source-equal-weight scale bug} (found after the v4a results): the first
\code{source\_equal\_weights} made each class sum to 1 instead of $n/2$ as for balanced weights, so LR's effective
regularisation was $\approx n/2$ times stronger (HistGB was also affected). Fixed to the balanced scale (relative weights
unchanged, \cref{sec:weights}); only 1a--1c were re-run: 1b identical, 12 rows of 1c changed, conclusion unchanged
(pre-fix results in commit \texttt{9120afb}). v4b1 ran after the fix.
\item \textbf{1e tie-break}: the pre-registration only said ``first in the grid''; the implementation fixed the full order
(algorithm, representation, grid) before the run.
\item \textbf{1D-CNN skipped} as pre-declared (no \texttt{torch}).
\item \textbf{v4b2}: a duplicate source (XB 1254$-$690 = 4U 1254$-$69) was fixed in the matching code before download; the
13 additional NSs result from executing the same rule correctly, not from a rule change; the download script
\file{21\_v4b2\_fetch\_process.py} is a copy of \file{04\_fetch\_process.py} that also scans AO1--AO4. The same-sample H-LR
comparison was added after the v4b2 results were seen (required by \S0 but missing in the script).
\item \textbf{v4b1}: raw files outside the repository, 6 download threads instead of 4; processing unchanged. Two descriptive
analyses (restriction to the v2 observations; per-source score plot) were decided before the v4b1 results.
\item \textbf{Approval}: the user approved v4 with ``start and finish full if no big problem'', so no stop was made before
the v4b1--v4b3 downloads, although the pre-registration foresaw one per sub-phase.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap} intervals keep the OOF predictions fixed and do not include the variability of model fitting or of
the nested selection; the nested source AUC is an approximation (shifted scores).
\item \textbf{Multiplicity}: 76 descriptive v4a combinations and 16 (+16) v4b1 combinations, uncorrected; the intervals above
0 (QDA; intensity models) are not interpreted.
\item \textbf{Bursts}: the automatic rule has false positives and misses; the visual verdicts were made by one person.
\item \textbf{HEXTE}: cluster B only; no features after 2009-12-14; $X_1$ is a cross-instrument count ratio; the overlap box is
a post hoc definition.
\item \textbf{v4b2}: many new NSs lie in the Galactic-centre region or in globular clusters (Terzan 5, Terzan 6, NGC 6440,
M15, NGC 1851), where the $\approx1^\circ$ PCA field may contain other sources; gain epoch and source identity are
confounded (XTE J1859+226 is entirely in epoch 4), so an epoch effect cannot be estimated.
\item \textbf{v2 source rule}: executed incompletely (13 NSs missed); the v1--v3 numbers were not changed and describe a
smaller NS sample.
\item \textbf{v4b1}: observation-level metrics are dominated by the sources with most observations (4U 1636$-$53: 1,329;
GRS 1915+105: 1,031); observations of the same source are strongly correlated.
\end{itemize}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Takeaway}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The two-colour ceiling is a property of the data, not of the models: more flexible algorithms (with honest nested
selection), burst cleaning, a 25--60\,keV extension and 17 times more observations all leave the classification of unseen
sources where H-LR put it. Adding sources helps only through the threshold. Single-spectrum information appears
exhausted at about source BA 0.8 and source AUC 0.92 on these sources, with hard-state BHs and hard NSs inseparable.
Phase 3 therefore turns to a different physical observable: fast X-ray variability.
