\chapter{Open questions for discussion}\label{ch:questions}
% =====================================================================================================
The following questions are where the advisor's judgement would change what is done next.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.6em]
\item \textbf{Is an observation-level prospective confirmation enough?} The hard-state timing gain was confirmed prospectively on new
observations (v13) but of the same 16 dynamically confirmed BHs (\cref{sec:synthladder}). Is that sufficient to state the result, or is a
source-level test on new transients required -- and if so, how many new BH sources would make it convincing?

\item \textbf{How should the $\nu_c$ result be framed?} The colour-conditional difference is consistent in size (0.3--0.6\,dex) but only
marginally significant in single data sets, and there is no Eddington-ratio or distance control (\cref{tab:synthnuc}). Should it be presented
as a possible mass-scaling signature, as a descriptive regularity, or not at all until distances for the $\approx60$ sources are collected?
Is collecting them worth the effort?

\item \textbf{Is the label-free residualisation too conservative?} When BHs cluster in colour, the kNN residualisation absorbs part of the
difference (v9b, v13b). Would a pre-registered matched or colour-window design be the better primary test for the next data set?

\item \textbf{RF or LR as the headline model?} The timing gain is stable only for RF (\cref{tab:synthgain}); RF was chosen post hoc in v12 and
pre-specified in v13. Is it acceptable to report RF as the headline model, given that the LR gain is the one that was pre-declared earlier?

\item \textbf{Which external data are realistic?} Insight-HXMT, AstroSat, future NICER or XRISM transients: which of them can provide
dynamically confirmed BHs with hard-state timing in the next year, and is script-based data access available?

\item \textbf{What is the publishable core?} The negative spectral results of Phases 1--2 (two colours are all one needs, with rigorous
source-level evaluation) are robust and may be useful on their own, independently of the timing results. Should they be written up
separately?
\end{enumerate}
