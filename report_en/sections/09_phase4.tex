\chapter{Phase 4 (v7--v8): where the colours fail, and why}\label{ch:phase4}
% =====================================================================================================

\begin{glance}{v7--v8 (+ v9c MAXI strata)}
\textbf{Question.} Phases 1--3 suggested that two colours separate BHs from NSs in soft states but not in hard states,
and that low-frequency variability fills the gap. Phase 4 makes this explicit and tests the alternatives: is the failure
a hard-state failure (variability-defined states)? Is any of it burst contamination (official MINBAR catalogue)? Do more
BHs (persistent HMXBs), higher frequencies (event mode, 4--1024\,Hz) or a different instrument (MAXI/GSC, 2--20\,keV)
change the picture? \\
\textbf{Pre-declared primaries} (one per sub-analysis, $\bigstar$ in the repository reports): v7b1 burst exclusion; v7a
soft-like $-$ hard-like AUC; v7b2 adding three persistent BHs; v7c1 reproduction of \citet{deBeurs2022}; v7c2 two LR
comparisons on MAXI; v8a timing inside the hard-like state; v8b the same on external sources; v8c event-mode
frequencies (exploratory after a failed gate); v8d MAXI 2--4\,keV gain after $N_\mathrm{H}$ correction. \\
\textbf{Data.} RXTE S1 (456 observations) with variability states; MINBAR DR1; Cyg X-1, LMC X-1, LMC X-3 (45 observations),
GS 1354$-$64 and SS 433 (23); 201 event-mode files (2.44\,GB); MAXI/GSC one-day light curves of 13 BHs, 48 non-pulsing
NSs and 39 pulsars. \\
\textbf{Verdict.} \textbf{Detected}: colours work in soft-like observations (AUC 0.954) and fail in hard-like ones (0.646;
difference $+0.309$ [$+0.099$, $+0.510]$); inside the hard-like state, low-frequency timing raises the AUC to 0.910
($+0.264$ [$+0.048$, $+0.455$]); on MAXI the 2--4\,keV colour survives an $N_\mathrm{H}$ correction ($+0.377$
[$+0.160$, $+0.595$]). \textbf{Not detected}: any effect of burst contamination; any effect of adding persistent BHs; the
hard-state timing gain on external sources ($+0.064$ [$-0.185$, $+0.367$]); the two LR comparisons on MAXI. Event-mode
high frequencies are exploratory and add nothing beyond Standard-1 timing. Both pre-registrations were written after all
earlier results; v8a was predictable from v5.
\end{glance}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
By the end of Phase 3 three statements were supported only indirectly: the colour classifier fails in the hard state,
timing helps precisely there, and type-I bursts do not matter. Each could be tested directly. Spectral states can be
assigned from variability alone (fractional rms; \citealp{RemillardMcClintock2006}), independently of the colours used
for classification; the official MINBAR burst catalogue \citep{Galloway2020} provides an independent burst record; the
persistent HMXB BHs add hard-state BHs (Cyg X-1) and soft-state BHs (LMC X-1, LMC X-3) that are not transients; event-mode
data reach the 500--1000\,Hz range where \citet{SunyaevRevnivtsev2000} found NS power and no BH power; and MAXI offers
long-term colours of many more sources from a different instrument, with a published machine-learning result
\citep{deBeurs2022} to reproduce. The analyses are grouped below by theme: data quality, states, timing within the hard
state, more BHs, higher frequencies, and an independent instrument (including the v9c MAXI strata, which were run with
Phase 5 but belong to this theme).

\subsection{Variability states (v7a)}\label{sec:p4states}
The state index (\cref{sec:state}) is the 0.1--4\,Hz fractional rms from the all-pairs Standard-1 cospectrum in 128-s rows
inside the spectral GTIs with $\ge2$ PCUs, after removing burst rows (MINBAR intervals in the field of view and v4
candidates from 20\,s before the burst; 143 rows), as the median over rows ($\ge3$ rows, otherwise ``unknown''). Following
Table~2 of \citet{RemillardMcClintock2006}, rms $>0.10$ is \emph{hard-like}, rms $<0.075$ \emph{soft-like}, otherwise
\emph{intermediate}. The band stops at 4\,Hz (RM06 use 0.1--10\,Hz), so the rms is lower and the hard-like assignment
conservative; the BH thresholds are applied to NSs as well. The pre-declared sanity check passed: XTE J1118+480 15/15
hard-like and the Z sources Cyg X-2 and GX 17+2 27/28 (96\%) soft-like (\file{results/v7a/sanity\_check.csv},
\cref{fig:p4rms}). The thresholds were not tuned.

\subsection{Burst contamination from MINBAR (v7b1)}
MINBAR DR1 \citep{Galloway2020} was downloaded from Monash Bridges (CC BY 4.0; 7111 bursts, 2288 from RXTE/PCA; md5
checked; SHA256 in \file{logs/downloads.jsonl}). The spectral GTIs (\code{STDGTI}, MET in TT) were converted to UTC MJD
(TT$-$UTC = 64.18\,s; round trip $<1$\,ms); the v4 burst peaks lie 1--9\,s after the MINBAR start times, confirming the
time systems agree. A burst interval is [start, start + duration] (duration missing $\Rightarrow$ 300\,s). \textbf{Primary rule
(revised after the matching, before any classification result)}: the interval overlaps a GTI \emph{and} the burst was
recorded in the same ObsID or originated within $1^\circ$ (the PCA field of view) of the target. The pre-registered literal
rule (any origin) also counts bursts seen by WFC/JEM-X 8--58$^\circ$ away (9 observations, 3 of them BH) and is kept as a
sensitivity set.

58 of the 456 observations are contaminated (all NS, 19 sources; 4U 1323$-$62 10, GS 1826$-$24 7, MXB 1658$-$298 6); the
literal rule gives 67. \Cref{tab:p4minbaragree} compares MINBAR with the v4 Standard-1 detector: agreement 99.1\% with the v4
visual verdicts and 94.5\% with the automatic rule. Each source misses bursts the other finds (\cref{fig:p4minbar}), so
neither alone is complete. Of the 58 contaminated time bins, 37 could be replaced by a clean observation from the same
bin; 21 had none (mostly frequent bursters with few eligible pointings).

\subsection{Event-mode data (v8c)}
Archival event files (\code{SE*.evt.gz}, processed in 2012) contain a \code{PCUID} column, so cross-PCU cospectra can be
formed at high frequency. File headers were inventoried by HTTP range requests (\file{results/v8c/event\_survey.csv}, 483
files). A file qualifies if its \code{DATAMODE} matches \verb|^E_\d+us_\d+M_0_\d+s$| (all channels), \code{TDDES2} contains
all six xenon layers and \code{TIMEDEL} $\le2^{-12}$\,s. Of the 311 hard-like observations of S1 and X, 201 had a qualifying
file (2.44\,GB; modes \code{E\_125us\_64M\_0\_1s} 327 files, \code{E\_125us\_64M\_0\_8s} 5, \code{E\_16us\_64M\_0\_8s} 5,
\code{E\_250us\_128M\_0\_1s} 4); the hard-like observations of GRS 1915+105 have only binned modes. The estimator
(\cref{sec:eventmode}) uses $2^{-12}$-s bins, 16-s segments inside the GTIs and away from bursts, all-pairs cospectra and
bands LC 1--4\,Hz, HF1 4--64\,Hz, HF2 64--512\,Hz, HF3 512--1024\,Hz and a null band 1536--2048\,Hz; quality cuts $\ge10$
segments and $|z_\mathrm{null}|\le5$ (0 of 148 S1 observations removed). S1-HF has 148 observations of 23 sources (6 BH);
X-HF 44 observations (2 BH, 11 NS).

\textbf{Gate IC4 failed.} The event-mode 1--4\,Hz rms should agree with the Standard-1 T$^*_3$ at Spearman $\ge0.90$; it gave
0.813 (0.818 after the estimator change below). The diagnosis (\file{results/v8c/ic4\_diagnosis.csv}), made before any v8c
model, is a noise ceiling rather than a bias: within the hard-like state the 1--4\,Hz rms spans a narrow range (IQR
0.11--0.14); T$^*_3$ itself has split-half Spearman 0.563 (Spearman--Brown full-data reliability 0.72), and two Standard-1
estimators agree only at 0.896; in the high-$S/N$ half the event and Standard-1 values agree at 0.978 with unit slope (median
ratio 0.94). Following the pre-registration, \textbf{all v8c results are exploratory}. The diagnosis also showed that the
pre-registered per-observation mean of per-segment ratios is unstable for faint sources (one Aql X-1 segment at 3.6\,c\,s$^{-1}$
\,PCU$^{-1}$ on 25\,c\,s$^{-1}$\,PCU$^{-1}$ background gave $-195$); it was replaced by the ratio of means (\cref{eq:ratioofmeans}),
with the old values kept in \code{*\_mean\_of\_ratios} columns.

\subsection{MAXI/GSC (v7c, v8d, v9c)}

within $0.1^\circ$ of an accretion-powered pulsar; pulsars (PSR, secondary task) = HMXBs with a pulse period in
\citet{Liu2006} plus \citet[Table 1]{PatrunoWatts2021}. MAXI source names were resolved with Sesame and matched within
$0.1^\circ$ (J-name position within $0.3^\circ$ as fallback when Sesame fails);

% and the decision log use Sesame positions within 0.1 deg (0.3 deg fallback). Code wins.
blended light curves (``with'' in the name) and ambiguous identifications were excluded. Data are the MAXI/GSC
standard one-day light curves in 2--4 (L), 4--10 (M) and 10--20\,keV (H), with the cuts of \citet{deBeurs2022}: every band
$\ge\sqrt3\,\sigma$ with positive errors, points with total $\ge$ mean + 10 std removed, $\ge100$ points per source. Passing:

\textbf{Absorption control (v8d).} HI4PI \citep{HI4PI2016} Galactic $N_\mathrm{H}$ from HEASoft \code{nh} (weighted average

$\log N_\mathrm{H}$; corrected fluxes $L'=L/t_L$ etc.\ and recomputed SC$'$, HC$'$, C4$'$, RelInt$'$. Gate IC5 passed
($t=0.864/0.979/0.997$ at $10^{22}$\,cm$^{-2}$, monotone); IC6 (uncorrected models reproduce v7c2) passed with maximum

\section{Models in this phase}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\section{Interpretation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{What the data support directly.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The two-colour classifier depends strongly on the state: AUC $\approx0.95$ on soft-like observations and $\approx0.65$ on
hard-like ones, difference $+0.31$ [$+0.10$, $+0.51$]; consistent across four models and after burst exclusion. The Phase 1
inference ``hard BHs and hard NSs overlap in colour'' is now a number with an interval.
\item Type-I burst contamination does not affect the Phase 1 conclusions: with the official MINBAR catalogue the 31 H-LR source
decisions are unchanged. MINBAR and the independent Standard-1 detector agree on 99.1\% of observations; each misses 2 bursts the
other finds.
\item Inside the hard-like state of S1, low-frequency timing raises the AUC from 0.65 to 0.91 (interval excludes 0), robustly across
models, estimators, nuisance control, burst exclusion and hard-only training; the information is in 0.016--1\,Hz, and the state gap
shrinks from 0.31 to 0.08. This is where the v5/v6 gain came from.
\item On external sources the hard-state gain is \emph{not} confirmed: the point estimate is positive (source-level BA $+0.13$) but the
interval is wide and one AMXP is pushed strongly towards BH.
\item Adding three persistent BHs does not change performance on the original 31 sources; all three are recognised as BHs when held
out, Cyg X-1 (hard-like) closest to the threshold.
\item At single-observation RXTE sensitivity, after null-band subtraction, broad-band 512--1024\,Hz power is detected in none of 6 BHs and
in 1 of 17 NSs; high frequencies add essentially nothing beyond the low-frequency features (exploratory).
\item The de Beurs et al.\ result reproduces exactly from their published inputs. With confirmed labels, standard MAXI products and
LOSO, LR has hardly any discriminating power; non-linear models reach $\approx0.8$ with two colours; the 2--4\,keV colour adds
information that survives a Galactic $N_\mathrm{H}$ correction and is present in every C4 tertile.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Possible explanations (not verified).}
\begin{itemize}
\item In soft states the disc dominates and the BH colours lie outside the NS track; in hard states both classes are dominated by
Comptonisation with similar 5--25\,keV shapes.
\item At equal hard colour, BHs show stronger $<1$\,Hz variability, consistent with lower characteristic frequencies (the $\nu_c$ test of
v6 was significant on S1 but not externally); amplitude and frequency are not separated here (taken up with Lorentzian fits in
Phase 6).
\item The external failure may reflect the BH composition (an HMXB, a gain-epoch-3 transient, an LMC source at the state threshold),
the AMXP-dominated NS sample (IGR J00291+5934 has strong low-frequency noise), and less room for improvement (external colour-only
hard-like AUC already 0.82 vs 0.65 on S1).
\item The SR2000 criterion may fail here because the sample is post-2000 (fewer PCUs, gain epoch 5), single observations last 1--3\,ks
while \citet{SunyaevRevnivtsev2000} combined observations with all five PCUs, and a broad 512--1024\,Hz band dilutes narrow kHz QPOs.
\item The MAXI 2--4\,keV information may reflect the disc temperature (cooler BH discs in thermal states, NS boundary-layer emission
harder; cf.\ \citealp{DoneGierlinski2003}); since the gain appears in all C4 tertiles it is not limited to thermal states. LR may
fail on MAXI because BHs lie on both sides of the NS distribution in the colour plane (soft and hard states), which is not linearly
separable.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Hypotheses that need more data.} A prospective confirmation of the frozen H+T model on more \emph{hard-state} confirmed BHs
-- the RXTE era is exhausted, so this needs NICER, Insight-HXMT, AstroSat or XRISM and a recalibrated timing feature (Phase 5); fits of
Lorentzian characteristic frequencies (Phase 6); stacked kHz searches with a model of the cross-PCU white component; state-resolved
MAXI analyses.

\section{Deviations and caveats}\label{sec:p4caveats}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Written after earlier results}: v7 after v1--v6 (user-approved), v8 after v1--v7 (self-approved); the state analysis tests
inferences already drawn, and v8a was predictable from v5.
\item \textbf{v7b1}: the primary burst rule (same ObsID or origin within $1^\circ$) replaced the literal rule after seeing the matching and
before any classification result; the MINBAR $\cup$ visual set was added after the cross-check; the comparison ``paired with v2 on
common sources'' was implemented as pairing with the 456-observation v2 OOF by source (same-observation comparisons also reported).
\item \textbf{v7b2}: post hoc check of the two high-leverage LMC X-3 observations (main analysis unchanged).
\item \textbf{v7c2 labels} (fixed before any C2 model): Liu+2007 LMXB pulse periods include burst oscillations, so LMXB pulsars come only
from \citet{PatrunoWatts2021}; HMXB pulsars from \citet{Liu2006} were added (the pre-registration named only Patruno \& Watts);
MAXI matching changed to Sesame-resolved names within $0.1^\circ$ (the J-name position within $0.35^\circ$ had mismatched
IGR J16358$-$4726 and 4U 1630$-$47); blends excluded; name fixes (V616 Mon, IGR J17480$-$2446, IGR J16358$-$4726).
\item \textbf{v7c2 computation}: secondary task only with LR and RF; the SVM inner selection uses grouped 5-fold instead of LOSO (estimated
$>6$\,h), decided before any SVM result; kNN used inner LOSO as pre-declared. Unspecified details fixed in code: standardisation of
kNN/SVM inputs, equal-prior kNN probabilities, SVM score $\sigma(f)$ with balanced weights.
\item \textbf{v8 gates and estimator}: the IC3 criterion ``squared HF $<0.01^2$ under pure Poisson'' was unattainable and replaced by
$|z|<3$, and a hand-computed segment-test expectation corrected (before models); IC4 failed $\Rightarrow$ v8c exploratory; the estimator
changed from mean of ratios to ratio of means after IC4 and before any v8c model; the null-band subtraction is post hoc.
\item \textbf{v8 tooling}: downloads switched from \texttt{urllib} ($\approx0.1$\,MB/s) to \texttt{curl} with the same logging; HTTP retries
added after one inventory connection error; a trailing \verb|\r| from a Windows CSV read in bash fixed in v8d before results. Only
two of the planned v8 figures were produced; the other results are in CSV files.
\item \textbf{States}: the index stops at 4\,Hz, uses BH thresholds for NSs and is an operational variability class, not a full state
assignment (no disc fraction, no photon index); 34 S1 observations (7 BH) are ``unknown''; external states use MINBAR-only burst
removal; the state of the faint LMC X-1 is uncertain.
\item \textbf{Small samples}: 7 BH sources in the S1 hard-like stratum (6 in S1-HF), 5 in the external hard-like stratum.
\item \textbf{MAXI}: standard products (2--4/4--10/10--20\,keV photon fluxes) instead of on-demand bands; one-day averages mix states;
globular-cluster light curves may blend sources; HI4PI gives only the Galactic atomic column (no molecular or intrinsic absorption, e.g.\
LMC X-1); RF as the v8d primary was chosen after v7; epochs differ between RXTE and MAXI.
\item \textbf{SS 433} is jet-dominated and a poor test of accretion-state classification (kept by rule and listed separately); SR2000 sources
overlap with this sample, so v8c is not an independent test of their criterion.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap} intervals keep predictions fixed.
\end{itemize}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Takeaway}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The failure of the colour classifier is a hard-state failure, and in the hard state of the training sources low-frequency
variability repairs most of it -- but that repair is not confirmed on external sources, the persistent BHs and higher frequencies add
nothing measurable, and bursts never mattered. On MAXI, with strict labels, linear colour models fail while the 2--4\,keV colour
carries information that is not absorption. The hard-state timing claim therefore needs a test on genuinely new observations with a
different instrument: Phase 5 takes it to NICER.
